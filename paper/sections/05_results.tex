\subsection{Comparison with State-of-the-Art}
\label{sec:sota}
% =============================================================================
% SOTA Table 1 — comparison with published methods on lumbar spine grading
% =============================================================================
We position our method against the best published lumbar spine MRI grading
results from 2021--2026 (Tab.~\ref{tab:sota}). Direct numerical comparison
on RSNA~2024 is intrinsically limited because the Kaggle metric is
sample-weighted log-loss with no public per-class F1 / AUC. To our
knowledge, no peer-reviewed paper applies a vision-language foundation
model to the RSNA~2024 dataset; ours is the first BiomedCLIP-based pipeline
on this benchmark.

The 0.945 / 0.957 numbers in Lin et al.~\cite{lin2024multiattention} and
Liu et al.~\cite{liu2025rsna} should be read with the table footnotes:
Lin et al.\ evaluate on a balanced binarised single-level L4--L5 subset,
and Liu et al.\ solve a different task — pixel-level segmentation of six
anatomical regions on the RSNA 2024 dataset rather than per-IVD severity
grading; the authors explicitly state their model ``does not directly
grade severity levels''.
The most directly comparable published row is
Nigru et al.~\cite{nigru2024spinenetv2}, which evaluates SpineNetV2 (our
shared backbone) on a 1\,747-disc external set spanning all five of our
label-space conditions. Their reported foraminal Bal.\,Acc of $\sim70\,\%$
($\kappa$ 0.46--0.47) is the realistic literature ceiling for sagittal-only
foraminal grading and contextualises our foraminal weakness as
intrinsic-to-modality rather than method-specific.

The split in Tab.~\ref{tab:spider_zeroshot} and Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}
is the central evidence for selective transfer: gains on disc-related
labels (where 3D volumetric context dominates) trade against losses on
vertebra/endplate labels (where 2D appearance is sufficient and the
RSNA-trained projection MLP over-specialises). We interpret this as a
characterisation of \emph{when} the hybrid integration is preferred to
a frozen 2D-only baseline, rather than a failure mode.

With $\sim 5$M trainable parameters, Hybrid frozen reaches Mean F1
macro $0.623$, exceeding both fully fine-tuned Vanilla ($0.610$) and
CBAM ($0.597$) baselines while training in $\sim 5$ minutes per epoch
on a single GPU. Full backbone fine-tuning (Hybrid unfreeze) does not
improve mean F1 — gains on three conditions are offset by a Pfirrmann
regression — supporting frozen as the better default
(Tab.~\ref{tab:spider_transfer}).
