## Recommendation + 1-line justification

**Accept (borderline; minor revision sufficient, not blocking).** The revision resolves every CRITICAL/MAJOR issue from R3 — prompts are now disclosed and fixed a priori, the off-the-shelf (OTS) BiomedCLIP baseline is now a full table column with honest numbers, and the regularizer claim is downgraded from "discovery" to a measured, statistically-bounded effect — and the authors have done something rare: they reported a result that *weakens their own headline* (OTS beats the hybrid on overall zero-shot mean F1). ## Strengths

- **S1. The OTS-loses-overall result is reported, not buried (Tab. tab:spider, Sec. 4.3).** OTS BiomedCLIP attains mean F1 0.394 vs. the hybrid's 0.362, and the text states this plainly ("its mean F1 over all eight labels (0.394) slightly exceeds the hybrid"). Reporting a baseline that beats your own method on the aggregate metric, and then correctly re-scoping the claim to *where* the method wins, is exactly the intellectual honesty a foundation-model reviewer wants to see and is the single best feature of this revision. It directly closes R3-W3 and R3-W4.

- **S2. The disc-subset win is now a substantiated, mechanistically-explained finding, not a qualitative assertion.** Bulging 0.613 vs 0.363, narrowing 0.586 vs 0.403, herniation 0.563 vs 0.532 (Tab. tab:spider) — all three disc-morphology labels show the RSNA-trained hybrid clearly above OTS, and the text attributes this to semantic proximity to the RSNA training schema. From a VLM standpoint this is the *correct* reading: RSNA supervision can only add transferable signal where the target concept overlaps the source label geometry. This is scientifically interesting, not cherry-picking — see calibration note below.

- **S3. Prompts disclosed and fixed a priori (Sec. 4.3).** The template "a magnetic resonance image of [label]" is now shown, with the binary anti-prompt construction exemplified ("lumbar disc herniation" vs. "no disc herniation"), and the no-leakage claim is explicit ("written a priori without tuning on SPIDER"). This closes R3-W1 (CRITICAL). The protocol is now reproducible.

- **S4. Regularizer claim downgraded to a measured effect + trivial-ensemble control run (Sec. 4.5/sec:disc).** The authors now (a) decline to claim the hybrid beats SpineNetV2 (parity, <1σ), (b) bound the one claim they make (Hybrid−CBAM +0.034 > 2σ), and (c) directly ran the logit-average control I asked for in R3-W8: averaging SpineNetV2+BMC-only reaches Mean F1 0.45 / Severe F1 0.18, below the learned-fusion hybrid (0.53/0.36), showing fusion is not reducible to averaging on the thresholded metric. This closes R3-W8.

- **S5. The AUC/F1 divergence is read correctly as a calibration, not a ranking, failure.** Spondylolisthesis AUC 0.807 with F1 0.028, Pfirrmann AUC 0.687 with F1 0.155 (Tab. tab:spider): the text correctly diagnoses "the embedding orders these grades while the fixed cosine threshold mis-calibrates them." This is the right VLM interpretation and is the empirical hook for the temperature concern (W2 below).

- **S6. 3-seed RSNA main table (Tab. tab:main) with mean±std across {42,123,456}.** Zero-shot/foundation papers routinely report single-seed point estimates; multi-seed with reported σ is above the bar for this venue and lets the regularizer and fusion claims rest on effect sizes rather than single runs.

## Weaknesses (issue / location / fix / severity)

**W1 — Single prompt template; no prompt ensembling. [MAJOR]**
*Location:* Sec. 4.3 ("Each class uses a fixed prompt 'a magnetic resonance image of [label]'"), Eq. eq:cosine. *Problem:* Prompts are now disclosed (good), but exactly ONE template is used. Prompt ensembling — averaging text embeddings over many templates — is the standard CLIP/BiomedCLIP variance-reduction control (cf. CLIP's 80-template ImageNet protocol), and its absence means the head-to-head OTS-vs-hybrid comparison and the entire per-label spread (0.018–0.613) are conditioned on one arbitrary phrasing. This matters *more* now that the comparison is OTS-vs-hybrid: both share the same single template, so the comparison is internally fair, but the *absolute* numbers and the disc-subset margins could shift with a better/ensembled template. A reviewer cannot tell whether the OTS-beats-hybrid-overall gap (0.394 vs 0.362) is robust or template-luck. *Fix:* Either (a) report mean±std of the OTS and Hybrid mean-F1 over a small template set (e.g., 5 paraphrases) — even two rows would establish the variance band — or (b) add one Limitations sentence acknowledging single-template fragility and that ensembling is unexplored. (a) is the rigorous fix and is cheap (frozen weights, inference-only).

**W2 — Temperature value undisclosed; OOD logit-scale reuse drives the F1/AUC divergence but is never quantified. [MAJOR]**
*Location:* Eq. eq:cosine ("s is the learned temperature ... inherited from BiomedCLIP pretraining"); Sec. 4.3 ("the fixed cosine threshold mis-calibrates them"). *Problem:* This is the one R3 issue (R3-W5) only partially addressed. The *numeric value* of s is still not printed, and — more importantly — the paper now itself diagnoses a calibration failure (AUC high, F1 collapsed on spondylolisthesis/Pfirrmann/Modic) that is *mechanistically the temperature's job*: s sets softmax sharpness, and for the 6 binary labels the argmax over {positive, negative} prompt is exactly where the two cosine similarities cross, which s rescales. The pretrain s is calibrated to PMC-15M, not to OOD lumbar crops. So the paper observes the symptom (mis-calibration) without connecting it to the obvious knob (inherited OOD temperature). This is a missed, self-contained analysis that would *strengthen* the paper: a per-label temperature/threshold sweep would likely recover much of the spondylolisthesis F1 (AUC 0.807 says the ranking is there). *Fix:* (i) Print the numeric s. (ii) Add one sentence connecting the F1/AUC divergence to the inherited OOD temperature, and either report sensitivity of one binary label's F1 to s (or to the decision threshold), or explicitly defer threshold calibration to future work as the cause of the collapsed-F1-high-AUC cases. This converts an unexplained anomaly into a controlled finding.

**W3 — Multiclass (Modic/Pfirrmann) prompt design is the weakest link and is under-described. [MINOR–MAJOR]**
*Location:* Sec. 4.3, Tab. tab:spider (Pfirrmann 5-class F1 0.155, Modic 4-class F1 0.018 hybrid / 0.071 OTS). *Problem:* For ordinal/graded labels the single binary-style template generalizes poorly: "a magnetic resonance image of [label]" for Pfirrmann grade k presumably becomes "...of Pfirrmann grade k disc degeneration" or similar, but the paper does not show the multiclass prompt strings. Fine-grained ordinal grading is precisely where BiomedCLIP is known to be weak (grade-specific captions are sparse in PMC-15M), and the near-chance Modic result (hybrid F1 0.018, AUC 0.454 — *below* random ranking) confirms it. The Modic collapse is partly explained (T2-FS marrow-signal physics — a good and correct point), but the Pfirrmann/Modic *prompt construction* is invisible, so the reader cannot separate "prompt design failure" from "concept genuinely absent in pretraining." *Fix:* Show the exact multiclass prompt template for Pfirrmann and Modic (one line each), and note that ordinal grades may need descriptor-based prompting (severity descriptors rather than grade indices) — frame the collapse as a known fine-grained-ordinal limitation of contrastive medical VLMs, which connects it to the literature rather than leaving it as an isolated bad number.

**W4 — "Label space becomes an input" still not anchored to the CLIP zero-shot lineage. [MINOR]**
*Location:* Sec. 3.1 ("the label space becomes an *input* to inference rather than a fixed architectural constant"). *Problem:* Carried over from R3-W7 — this italicized framing is the defining property of CLIP-style open-vocabulary classification (Radford et al. 2021), not a novel mechanism. The references list contains zhang2023biomedclip and lu2024radclip but, to my reading, no general CLIP citation at this sentence. A VLM reviewer reads the un-cited framing as implicit over-claiming of a standard mechanism. *Fix:* One half-sentence + a CLIP citation ("a standard property of contrastive image-text models, here applied to cross-*dataset* lumbar label-space extension"). The genuine novelty is the cross-dataset *application* and the regularizer finding — lean on those, which the revised contribution list (now ordered by multi-seed evidence) already does well.

**W5 — "2.5D" terminology + per-slice-independence justification still under-stated. [MINOR]**
*Location:* Sec. 3.3 "BiomedCLIP branch (frozen, 2.5D)", Eq. eq:slicepool. *Problem:* Carried over from R3-W6, partially addressed (the attention-pool motivation "up-weight the slices in which a lesion is most clearly visible" is now present). Two residual VLM-specific points: (1) "2.5D" conventionally implies orthogonal-plane or adjacent-slice stacking; here it is per-slice 2D forward passes + a learned attention pool, which is closer to a multiple-instance attention pool over slice tokens — the term slightly oversells the cross-slice modeling inside the frozen branch. (2) The paper still does not state *why* per-slice independence is acceptable: it is, because the CBAM-3D branch carries the volumetric context and the frozen branch only supplies a per-slice semantic prior. *Fix:* One sentence stating the division of labor (3D context ← CBAM branch; per-slice semantic prior ← frozen branch), and consider "per-slice 2D + attention pooling (a 2.5D treatment)" to avoid over-claiming. Turns an apparent weakness into a justified design choice.

**W6 — Trivial-ensemble control is 2-seed while everything else is 3-seed; AUC/AUPRC story now cuts against learned fusion. [MINOR]**
*Location:* Sec. 4.5/sec:disc ("two-seed mean"), and the new finding that the trivial ensemble beats learned fusion on AUC/AUPRC. *Problem:* From a representation-learning view, the control now shows a genuinely mixed picture: learned concat-MLP fusion wins on *thresholded* Severe F1 (0.36 vs 0.18) but the trivial average wins on *ranking* (AUC/AUPRC). The paper resolves this in the hybrid's favor by appealing to the clinical operating point ("better ranking but worse thresholded Severe decisions"), which is a defensible argument — but it is built on a 2-seed control against 3-seed mains, an inconsistency that a careful reviewer will flag. Also, the AUC/AUPRC result is itself evidence for the same temperature/threshold issue as W2: averaging is better-calibrated for ranking, learned fusion is better at the (mis-set) threshold. *Fix:* Run the third seed for the ensemble control for consistency, and tie the "averaging wins on ranking" observation to the calibration discussion (W2) — the two are the same phenomenon and currently read as separate. If the third seed is infeasible, label the control explicitly as a 2-seed sanity check, not a measured comparison.

## Calibration note (novelty)

**Is the label-space extension still a meaningful contribution of THEIR method, now that OTS beats the hybrid overall?** Partially, and the revision now frames it correctly. The *generic capability* (cosine zero-shot to a new label vocabulary) is inherited CLIP behavior — any text-aligned encoder has it, and OTS BiomedCLIP demonstrably has it *better* on the aggregate. So the contribution is NOT "we enable zero-shot" (that is inherited). The defensible contribution is narrower and now honestly stated: **RSNA-supervised fusion adds transferable signal on the label subset semantically close to the training schema (disc bulging/herniation/narrowing), at the cost of the off-schema labels** — i.e., supervised transfer is *selective*, helping where source and target concepts overlap and hurting where they diverge. That is a real, mechanistically-grounded finding about *when* domain-specific supervision helps vs. hurts a frozen foundation prior, and it is scientifically interesting precisely *because* it is selective and the authors quantified both sides. It is **not cherry-picking** — the win is on a coherent, a-priori-identifiable semantic cluster (disc morphology = the RSNA training concepts), not an arbitrary best-3-of-8, and the authors report the losing labels and the losing aggregate in the same table.

**Regularizer claim:** A known effect (frozen foundation features resist co-trained overfitting) *demonstrated* cross-dataset for this architecture with 3-seed effect sizes — correctly downgraded from "discovery" to "we read this as descriptive evidence." The trivial-ensemble control now partially undercuts the *learned-fusion-specifically* justification on ranking metrics (averaging wins on AUC/AUPRC), so "learned fusion" is justified over averaging *only at the thresholded clinical operating point*, not in general. The paper's hedged framing matches this; it should not be strengthened.

**Positioning ("first BiomedCLIP on RSNA / first RSNA→SPIDER zero-shot"):** Defensible as a *first application/data-point* claim, qualified with "to our knowledge" and "no prior peer-reviewed work" (Sec. 1, Sec. 4.3) — appropriate hedging. This is an incremental application of an existing model (BiomedCLIP) to a new dataset pair, which is fine for a venue like MIWAI provided it is not oversold as a methodological novelty; the revised contribution ordering (regularizer finding first, hybrid architecture second, label extension third) correctly does not lead with the application claim.
